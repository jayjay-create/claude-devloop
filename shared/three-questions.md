**At most three questions in a round, and something that needs weighing goes
alone.** Three is a ceiling, not a target. Putting several real questions in
one block means the user skims and takes the recommendations, which gathers no
decisions at all, only agreement with your own answers, and teaches them to
wave through the one that mattered.

**In this interview, every decision about the idea is a question to them with
your recommendation**, even one a later task could change cheaply. You settle
yourself only what the first of the two tests under "How to ask" sorts out —
what has a right answer to look up in the repository, the documents or on the
web, what follows from what they have already said, what is a convention of
this workflow — and what the project's own rules already settle. Where you
cannot tell whether a point is settled so, it is a question. The second test
under "How to ask", whether being wrong is expensive, does not hold in this
interview, nor does the paragraph there on deciding everything else yourself
beyond what the first test sorts out, and a question answerable in one word
from your own recommendation is a question all the same. The interview ends
only when nothing is left silently assumed; a decision taken for them is such
an assumption, and every later task builds on what it settles. Since 18
October 2026, the roadmap entry of that date; until then both tests held here
and whatever one word from your recommendation could settle was decided, and
on 16 October 2026 a run settled the whole frontier itself and put no question
until the person asked for them.

What you settled yourself in a round you say in one line — "taking X, Y and Z
as given; say if any of those is wrong" — where its questions are put, since
they rest on it and an answer can overturn it: through the widget, in the
question field of the first of them, before its question line; as text, in the
message that puts them, before the first. A round that asks nothing hands its
line on to the next question put.
